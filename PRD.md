# Product Requirements Document: Grandma Jeong's Cookbook

## 1. Product Overview

Grandma Jeong's Cookbook is a simple family recipe website created to digitally preserve Julie Jeong's handwritten recipes. The site helps family members, including Grandma Julie, find trusted recipes quickly, cook familiar meals, and keep family food traditions available for future generations.

The product should feel personal, warm, colorful, and easy to use. Because the primary client has difficulty navigating complex websites, the experience must prioritize large readable text, simple navigation, clear page labels, and a low number of distracting controls.

## 2. Client

- **Client:** Julie Jeong
- **Relationship:** Grandmother of the site creator
- **Primary need:** Preserve and organize handwritten family recipes in a digital format
- **Design preference:** Simple, easy to navigate, readable, colorful, and inspired by Hawaii

## 3. Problem Statement

Grandma Julie's recipes are currently stored on paper. Paper recipes can be misplaced, damaged, difficult to search through, and hard to share with family members. Family members need a centralized digital archive where they can browse recipes and follow clear cooking instructions.

## 4. Goals

- Preserve Grandma Julie's recipes digitally for long-term family use.
- Make recipes easy to find.
- Provide readable, step-by-step recipe pages that are comfortable for elders to use.
- Share Grandma Julie's story and cooking background.
- Create a Hawaii-inspired visual style that feels personal, colorful, and family-centered.

## 5. Non-Goals

- The site does not need food news, recipe-of-the-day content, advertisements, or unrelated cooking articles.
- The site does not need public user accounts or social networking features.
- The site does not need advanced shopping list, nutrition tracking, or meal planning tools for the first version.
- The site does not need to support recipes outside Grandma Julie's family collection.

## 6. Target Users

### Primary Users

- Julie Jeong, who needs an easy way to access her recipes online.
- Family members who want to cook Grandma Julie's recipes.

### Secondary Users

- Future family members who may use the site as a family archive.
- Relatives who want to learn about Grandma Julie's background and cooking traditions.

## 7. User Needs

- As Grandma Julie, I want the website to be simple so I can find recipes without getting confused.
- As an elder family member, I want large readable text so I can comfortably read ingredients and instructions.
- As a family member, I want each recipe to include time and difficulty so I know what to expect before cooking.
- As a family member, I want photos or visual references so I can recognize each dish.
- As a future family member, I want to read about Grandma Julie so the recipes feel connected to family history.

## 8. Core Features

### 8.1 Home Page

The home page must introduce Grandma Jeong's Cookbook and explain that the site preserves Grandma Julie's family recipes. It should provide clear entry points to the recipe list and the about page.

**Requirements:**

- Display the site name prominently.
- Include a short description of the purpose of the site.
- Include clear links or buttons to browse recipes and learn about Julie.
- Highlight a small set of family favorite recipes.

### 8.2 About Page

The about page must share Grandma Julie's background and explain why her recipes are meaningful to the family.

**Requirements:**

- Include Grandma Julie's name.
- Describe her connection to Hawaii, family, and home cooking.
- Explain that the site preserves recipes that were previously handwritten or stored on paper.
- Keep the writing warm, personal, and easy to read.

### 8.3 Recipe Listing Page

The recipe listing page must show all available recipes in a scannable format.

**Requirements:**

- Display all recipes in a consistent layout.
- Include recipe name, category, estimated time, difficulty, short description, and ingredient tags.
- Include a visual placeholder or photo area for each recipe.
- Provide a clear link to each individual recipe page.

### 8.4 Individual Recipe Pages

Each recipe page must provide the information needed to cook the dish.

**Requirements:**

- Include recipe title.
- Include category, estimated time, and difficulty.
- Include ingredients.
- Include step-by-step instructions.
- Include serving notes, family notes, or tips when available.
- Include a clear path back to the full recipe list.

### 8.5 Recipe Photos

The site should eventually include photos of finished dishes for visual guidance.

**Requirements:**

- Use photos when available.
- Use readable placeholders when photos are not yet available.
- Maintain a consistent layout so missing photos do not make the site feel unfinished.

## 9. Content Requirements

### Existing Content

- Handwritten recipes from Grandma Julie.
- Background information about Grandma Julie.
- Initial recipe names and dish descriptions.
- Sketches for the proposed layout.

### Needed Content

- Photos of finished recipes.
- Any missing measurements or cooking details from handwritten recipes.
- Optional family notes, memories, or tips for individual dishes.

## 10. Design Requirements

- Use a simple layout with minimal distractions.
- Use large, easy-to-read text.
- Use clear navigation with a small number of main links.
- Use colorful visual accents inspired by Hawaii.
- Avoid overwhelming users with too many buttons, articles, ads, or unrelated sections.
- Make recipe cards and recipe pages easy to scan.
- Ensure the site works well on desktop and mobile screens.

## 11. Accessibility Requirements

- Use semantic HTML headings and landmarks.
- Provide descriptive page titles and meta descriptions.
- Ensure text has strong contrast against the background.
- Use labels for search inputs.
- Make links and buttons easy to identify and click.
- Avoid relying on color alone to communicate important information.

## 12. Success Metrics

- Users can find a known recipe within one minute.
- Users can understand the purpose of the site from the home page.
- Grandma Julie can navigate from the home page to a recipe without assistance.
- Family members can browse all recipes from one central recipe list.
- Recipe pages contain enough information to cook the dish without needing the original paper recipe.

## 13. Constraints

- The site should remain simple and lightweight.
- The first version can use static HTML, CSS, and local recipe pages.
- Photos may be added later as they become available.

## 14. Inspiration

- **Food Network Recipes:** Useful recipe page structure with time and difficulty information, but too many buttons and distractions for this project.
- **Simply Recipes:** Preferred for its simpler layout and ability to save liked recipes, though this family website should not include unrelated food news or recipe-of-the-day content.

## 15. Future Enhancements

- Add a favorite or liked recipe feature for family members.
- Add printable recipe pages.
- Add real dish photos for every recipe.
- Add filters by category, ingredient, time, or difficulty.
- Add a private family-only version if sensitive family content is added.
- Add a way for family members to submit recipe memories or cooking tips.

## 16. Acceptance Criteria

- The site includes a home page, about page, recipe listing page, and individual recipe pages.
- The main navigation is visible and consistent across pages.
- Recipe cards include name, category, time, difficulty, short description, tags, and a link to the recipe.
- Each individual recipe page includes ingredients and instructions.
- Text is readable for older users.
- The visual style feels personal, warm, and Hawaii-inspired.
